## What these runs do and do not show

- **Wine, not Windows 98.** The calls and their arguments are the game's. What a call does is Wine 11.20's DirectSound: that a copy starts with the original's volume, what `GetStatus` returns and when a buffer that is not looping counts as stopped.
- **Open: copies that never get a `SetVolume`.** Under Wine a copy made while the original is at -10000 is silent until the game sets its volume, and most copies of the looping world sounds never get such a call (world 1: 11 of the 13 copies of forest3.WAV). Microsoft's page for `DuplicateSoundBuffer` has a remark about a known issue: a duplicate plays at full volume until its volume is set to a value different from the original's ([IDirectSound8::DuplicateSoundBuffer](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/previous-versions/windows/desktop/ee418041(v=vs.85))). It does not say which Windows versions or drivers. If the Windows of 2000 did that with these buffers, the same copies were loud there instead of silent. Not established here; it needs a start of the original on Windows 98 (86Box).
- **Return values.** Wine's trace prints arguments, not HRESULTs. It prints a warning on every failing path of the buffer methods, and there was none, so every call is recorded as "ok". `GetStatus`, `GetFrequency`, `Lock` and the reference counts are real values.
- **Time.** There is no sound device. Wine's stream goes through ALSA's file plugin into `pace.py`, which takes it at the played rate; the mixer rate in the first table shows the play cursors ran at real time (48000 frames a second wanted). With ALSA's plain null device they run about 3.6 times too fast and every sound that is not looping ends early. Trace lines are stamped in whole milliseconds.
- **Frame rate.** The game has no frame limiter and ran at 170 to 600 frames a second on software OpenGL, so the number of `SetPan` and `SetVolume` calls says nothing about a real PC. Values and their times do. The clock of the run starts at 120 s, as on a PC that was just switched on.
- **First frame.** Observed: in the first frame after loading most world sounds are `Play`ed at -10000 and get their value in the second frame, 0.02 to 0.07 s later, and one copy can be at its full value for that one frame only (world 1: #182, -1625 from 0.001 to 0.054 s). Inferred, not checked in the code: the first frame works from a listener position that is not the camera's yet.
- **Run to run.** Two starts of world 1 give the same calls with times a few milliseconds apart; values that depend on the frame clock differ slightly (the music ends its fade at -988 or -989; rcland1.wav -2170 and -2163).
- **Settings.** Levels depend on the player's volume settings, here 80 and 80, and on `Volume.ini`. Difficulty 1; other difficulty levels were not run. The volume formula in this file is fitted to the trace and the INI values (exact to the unit for every file that reached its full value); the distance part was not worked out.
- **Not captured:** the bytes written into the buffers (only their sizes, equal to the WAV data chunks), anything after 42 s, the menu, the results screen, the end of a world and the shutdown (the runs end by killing the process, so the release of the original buffers is not in the trace). Walking was tried in world 1 only, 10 s forward; the player walks into an obstacle after about 2 s and the footsteps continue.
